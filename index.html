Riddles

A few of my favorite brain teasers. Give each one a shot before peeking at the answer.

River Crossing
A man has to get a fox, a chicken, and a sack of corn across a river. He has a rowboat that can carry only him and one other thing. If the fox and the chicken are left together, the fox will eat the chicken. If the chicken and the corn are left together, the chicken will eat the corn. How does the man do it?
See Answer
Take the chicken across first (the fox and corn are safe together). Go back, take the fox across, then bring the chicken back. Leave the chicken, take the corn across and leave it with the fox. Finally, return for the chicken.
Three Light Switches
A lightbulb is inside a closed closet — you can't see if it's on or off. Outside are three switches, one of which controls the bulb. You can flip the switches however you want, but once you open the door you can no longer touch them. How do you figure out which switch controls the light?
See Answer
Flip switch 1 and wait a few minutes. Flip switch 1 back off, then immediately flip switch 2. Open the door. If the light is on, switch 2 controls it. If it's off, feel the bulb — if it's warm, switch 1 controls it; if cold, it's switch 3.
10 Bags of Gold Coins
You have 10 bags of 1,000 coins each. One bag is full of forgeries. Real coins weigh 1 gram; forgeries weigh 1.1 grams. You can visit the scale only once and get one accurate weight. How do you identify the bag of forgeries?
See Answer
Take 1 coin from bag 1, 2 from bag 2, and so on up to 10 from bag 10. Weigh them all together. Without forgeries the total would be 55 grams. The excess — e.g. 55.3 grams means 3 forgeries — tells you exactly which bag it is.
Burning Rope
You have two oil-soaked ropes, each of which takes exactly 1 hour to burn — but they don't burn at a constant rate. Using only a lighter, how can you measure exactly 45 minutes?
See Answer
Light rope 1 from both ends and rope 2 from one end at the same time. When rope 1 burns out (30 min), immediately light the other end of rope 2. It will burn out in exactly 15 more minutes — 45 minutes total.
Crates of Apples & Oranges
Three crates are labeled "A" (apples), "O" (oranges), and "A+O" (both) — but all three labels are wrong. You may pick one crate and have one fruit pulled from it. How do you determine what's in all three crates?
See Answer
Pick from the "A+O" crate. Since all labels are wrong, it must be either all apples or all oranges. If you pull an apple, that crate is apples — so the "A" crate must be oranges, and the "O" crate must be both. Reverse the logic if you pull an orange.
Four Men in Hats
Four men are buried neck-deep in a line with a wall between men 1 and 2. Each wears one of two black and two white hats. They can only look forward, can't speak, and must identify their own hat color or everyone gets shot. After one minute, one man calls out with certainty. Which one, and how does he know?
See Answer
Man 3 calls out. He can see man 2's hat. Man 4 can also see men 2 and 3 but stays silent — which tells man 3 that men 2 and 3 are wearing different colors. Since man 3 can see man 2's hat, he knows his own must be the opposite color.
